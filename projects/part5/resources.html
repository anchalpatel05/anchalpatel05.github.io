Government & Official Resources

Free, reliable, and often overlooked by students.

🎓

FAFSA

studentaid.gov

Apply for federal student aid every year. Covers grants, work-study, and low-interest loans.

📋

studentaid.gov

studentaid.gov

Manage your federal student loans, apply for repayment plans, and track your aid history.

📄

AnnualCreditReport.com

annualcreditreport.com

Get your free credit report from all three bureaus once per year. Federally mandated — it's free.

🛡️

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

consumerfinance.gov

Government agency with free financial education tools, calculators, and complaint resolution.

💰

IRS Free File

irs.gov/freefile

File your taxes for free if you earn under $79,000. Many students qualify. Don't pay to file.

🔍

BenefitsFinder

benefits.gov

Find government benefits you may qualify for — food assistance, housing, health insurance, and more.

12 Quick Money Wins

Pick three and start this week.

1

Eat before grocery shopping — hunger leads to impulse buys

2

Use incognito mode when shopping online — prices can vary

3

Buy textbooks used, rent them, or find PDFs through your library

4

Negotiate your phone plan — carriers offer student deals not advertised

5

Walk or bike for trips under 2 miles instead of ridesharing

6

Cook in batches on Sundays to avoid expensive last-minute takeout

7

Use the campus gym instead of paying for an off-campus membership

8

Check Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist before buying furniture new

9

Apply for every scholarship — small ones ($500–2K) go unclaimed constantly

10

Turn off auto-renew on all subscriptions and review them quarterly

11

Get a library card — free ebooks, audiobooks, movies, and magazines

12

Open a high-yield savings account and put your emergency fund there
